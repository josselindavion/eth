### 1. Results of `taskI_20261013-1554`

All three runs completed. Fetched with `fetch_study.py taskI_20261013-1554 --clean` (restart files deleted on Euler; the study still takes 2.3 GB there, mostly the three `.odb`). Every CSV has 15 columns and the expected duration (3.652 / 2.134 / 1.834 ms). On the PC, `matplotlib`, `pandas`, `numpy` and later `scipy` had to be installed (`py -m pip install --user ...`; one installation was interrupted because two commands were pasted at once).

First reading with `plot_case.py` (mean rate between 0.5 and 25 %): 98 / 504 / 889 s⁻¹. A closer look corrected two first impressions:

- **Strain rates.** The mean over 0.5–25 % includes the start of loading. On the steady part the rates match the targets: 85 → 105 → 69 s⁻¹ (100 s⁻¹, end of pulse at ≈ 18 %), 529 → 568 s⁻¹, 1050 → 1108 s⁻¹. The rate slowly increases during the test because the specimen force drops (necking), so the specimen brakes the bench less. No change of the striker velocity needed.
- **Equilibrium at 1000 s⁻¹.** A point-by-point criterion |F_in − F_out| / F_mean < 5 % said "equilibrium only at 1.646 ms", i.e. at 30 % strain. In fact the two forces overlap well but the ratio oscillates very fast (±5–15 %), and a single spike breaks the criterion.

Other observations:
- **Necking:** the force is maximum around 5 % strain (≈ 7.5 kN, i.e. ≈ 1000 MPa engineering stress) and drops to ≈ 3.4 kN at 30 %.
- **Output bar gauge:** strongly oscillating at 500 and 1000 s⁻¹ (peak 16 kN for 7.5 kN real) and delayed by ≈ 0.1 ms (travel over the 400 mm to the gauge).
- **Start of loading:** the force rises, drops back to zero, then rises again (≈ 0.04 ms): most likely the specimen shoulders hitting the pockets, since the specimen lies in them without pre-load.

### 2. What the deliverables must show

Checked in the lab papers how the experiments are evaluated:
- force from the strain gauge on the output bar, 400 mm from the specimen, sampled at 400 kHz (Beerli 2026), low-pass filtered at **100 kHz** (DAVID software, Roth 2015);
- strain from DIC (high-speed camera at 100–200 kHz);
- numerical check of equilibrium in Beerli 2026 (fig. 5c): force of the output gauge "transported" to the specimen compared with the forces in sections at both ends of the specimen.

Decisions:
- 30 % at 100 s⁻¹ is impossible with the 5 m striker → written as a result;
- simulated forces and strain are filtered exactly like the experimental gauge signal (4th-order Butterworth, 100 kHz, zero phase), **before** computing R. Abaqus writes one point per µs and contains oscillations of a few µs that the experiment cannot see; filtering both the same way compares like with like;
- deliverables = figures + English text, to be integrated later into a PDF report.

Effect of the filter (max |R| after 5 % strain / strain from which |R| < 5 % holds until 30 %):

| Case | Unfiltered | Filtered 100 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| 100 s⁻¹ | 1.1 %, from 0.9 % | 0.9 %, from 0.9 % |
| 500 s⁻¹ | 5.5 %, from 10 % | 3.8 %, from 2.5 % |
| 1000 s⁻¹ | 15.5 % | 13.2 %, not maintained |

At 1000 s⁻¹ the remaining difference is real and slower: |R| < 5 % from ≈ 4 to 12 % strain, ≈ 8 % around 13–15 %, then 10–13 % after 20 %. The absolute difference stays at 0.3–0.6 kN, but the force halves during necking, so the ratio grows.

### 3. `scripts/post/report_taskI.py`

Reads the CSVs of a study and writes into `results/<study>/report/`:

| Output | Content |
|---|---|
| `T1_conditions.csv` | one line per run: striker velocity, incident strain, achieved rate (mean slope 5–25 %), time to 30 %, max strain, max force and strain at max force, equilibrium onset (strain and time), max \|R\| and max \|ΔF\| after 5 %, max error of the output gauge vs specimen force, hourglass ratio |
| `F1_equilibrium` | R (filtered) vs engineering strain, three rates, ±5 % band |
| `F2_forces_<SPEC>` | F_in, F_out and output gauge force shifted by 400 mm / 4871 m/s = 82 µs, one panel per rate (as Beerli fig. 5c) |
| `F3_stress_strain` | "measured" engineering stress-strain curve (output gauge / A0, A0 = 5 × 1.5 = 7.5 mm²) vs exact specimen section force |
| `F4_strain_rate` | strain rate vs strain, target rates as dotted lines |
| `F5_striker_velocity` | chart striker velocity → strain rate (formula + simulated points), region where 30 % is unreachable (ε̇ < 0.30 / 2.12 ms ≈ 142 s⁻¹) |
| `report.md` | English text with the numbers computed automatically, table and figures |

Each figure is saved as PDF (vector, for the report) and PNG.

**Results (UT19):**

| Target | Achieved | 30 % | Equilibrium (\|R\| < 5 %) | Max \|R\| after 5 % | Max gauge error |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 s⁻¹ | 105 s⁻¹ | no, 18.5 % max | from 0.9 % strain | 0.9 % | 3 % |
| 500 s⁻¹ | 546 s⁻¹ | in 0.63 ms | from 2.5 % | 3.8 % | 15 % |
| 1000 s⁻¹ | 1073 s⁻¹ | in 0.33 ms | not maintained | 13.2 % (ΔF ≤ 0.6 kN) | 39 % |

Hourglass energy < 0.5 % of the internal energy in all runs.

### 4. Storage of the results in the repository

Before, the fetched results landed in `runs/_submit/<study>/`, mixed with the input files sent to Euler. New organisation:

```
results/<study>/
├── <case>/   _history.csv, _paras.inp, .sta, .msg, _inventory.txt, _overview.png  (+ .odb on the PC only)
├── <study>_summary.csv
└── report/   T1, F1–F5 (.pdf + .png), report.md
```

- `fetch_study.py` now unpacks into `results/<study>/`; option `--odb all` copies every `.odb`; `extract_study.sh` skips runs already extracted and packs the `_paras.inp` (striker velocity of each run).
- Versioned: CSV, figures, logs (`.sta`, `.msg`), parameters. Not versioned: the generated input files (`runs/`, regenerated by `make_run.py`) and the `.odb` (≈ 0.7 GB each; GitHub refuses files > 100 MB), which stay on the PC next to the other results.
