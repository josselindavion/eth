### 2. Answers for the UT19

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Equilibrium at 100 s⁻¹ | yes, from 0.9 % strain (max \|R\| 0.9 % afterwards) |
| Equilibrium at 500 s⁻¹ | yes, from 2.5 % strain (max \|R\| 3.8 % after 5 %) |
| Equilibrium at 1000 s⁻¹ | partial: \|R\| < 5 % from ≈ 4 to 12 % strain, up to 13 % afterwards (ΔF ≤ 0.6 kN = 8 % of the max force; the ratio grows because the force drops during necking) |
| Striker velocity for 500 / 1000 s⁻¹ | 7.69 m/s → 546 s⁻¹, 30 % in 0.63 ms; 14.2 m/s → 1073 s⁻¹, 30 % in 0.33 ms |
| 30 % at 100 s⁻¹ | impossible with the 5 m striker: 2.48 m/s gives 105 s⁻¹ but the 2.12 ms pulse ends at 18.5 % strain; 30 % requires at least ≈ 140 s⁻¹ |
| Measurement quality | the output gauge deviates from the specimen force by up to 3 / 15 / 39 % of the max force at 100 / 500 / 1000 s⁻¹: even when the specimen is in equilibrium (500 s⁻¹), the measured curve oscillates |

F4 shows that the strain rate is not perfectly constant: it increases slightly during the test because the specimen force drops after the maximum (necking).

### 3. Limitations to state in the report

- The DP1000 model is rate independent: in the experiments the force will be higher at high rates, so a slightly higher striker velocity than these values will be needed.
- "Loading speed" in the task was interpreted as the striker velocity (the setting of the test). If the speed imposed on the specimen (m/s) is meant, it can be extracted from the same runs.

### 5. Origin of the material data

The DP1000 hardening curve was defined on 9 October in `scripts/pre/make_materials.py`, from Beerli et al. 2026 table 4 (Swift–Voce: A = 1395 MPa, ε0 = 1e-6, n = 0.0892, k0 = 739 MPa, Q = 301 MPa, β = 102, αSV = 0.5; E = 195 GPa, ν = 0.33, ρ = 7850 kg/m³), checked again in the paper today. This is the material Beerli uses for his own simulation of the bench (§2.5.1: rate-independent, von Mises, isotropic hardening similar to the DP1000). Not used: the anisotropic yield surface, the rate and temperature dependence (neural network), the thermal properties and the fracture model. All material data and these choices are gathered in `docs/materials.md` (new section "Ce qu'on a pris dans Beerli…").
