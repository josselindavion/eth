Understood the issue of the high frequencies of the yield stress (that can be cut by DAVID's low-band width filter) + the yield stress happens sometimes before the specimen equilibrium (the strain velocity isn't constant at the very beginning).
The impulsion has a rising time before reaching its final value (around 60 micro seconds), and when the specimen reaches yield stress, the strain velocity is still evolving ; we measure a yield stress for a reeal velocity different from the nominal strain velocity that we chose.
Dynamic equilibrium of the specimen : when the pusher starts to push, only the right side of the specimen feels it, the information (elastic wave) has to go through the whole specimen so that the other side of the specimen staerts to be pushed : before 4 transit times, the forces in the specimen are not uniform.
The rise time of the impulsion helps being a bit more uniform a the start.
After yield strain, the elastic waves are slower : equilibrium is thus harder to get during plastic phase.

We need the whole transit time (after which the forces are uniform in the specimen) to be shorter than the yield strain time otherwise the force recorded won't be the real force in the whole specimen.
