# Task I - Force equilibrium and loading speeds in the SHPB with load inversion device

Study `taskI_20261013-1554`, specimens: UT19. Generated by `scripts/post/report_taskI.py`; all numbers below are computed from the simulation outputs.

## 1. Simulation and signal processing

The complete bench (5 m striker, 6.01 m input bar, load inversion device, output bar and specimen) is modelled in Abaqus/Explicit using the symmetry plane (half model), without mass scaling. The specimen material is DP1000 with a rate-independent von Mises / isotropic hardening law: differences between strain rates therefore come from wave propagation and inertia only, not from material rate sensitivity.

The simulated signals are processed as in the experiments. Forces and strain are low-pass filtered (4th-order Butterworth, 100 kHz cut-off, zero phase), the cut-off used for the output bar strain gauge signal in the laboratory. The axial force is taken in two cross-sections at both ends of the specimen parallel section (F_in on the load inversion device side, F_out on the output bar side) and, as in the experiment, from the strain gauge on the output bar (F = A E ε). The gauge signal is shifted in time by 400 mm / 4871 m/s = 82 µs to the specimen (no dispersion correction). The engineering strain is measured by a 12 mm virtual extensometer on the parallel section, equivalent to the DIC extensometer.

Force equilibrium is quantified by R = (F_in - F_out) / F_mean with F_mean = (F_in + F_out) / 2. The specimen is considered in equilibrium when |R| < 5 %. The reported equilibrium strain is the strain from which |R| stays below this limit until 30 % strain (or until the end of loading). The achieved strain rate is the slope of the extensometer strain between 5 % strain and min(25 %, 0.8 x maximum strain), i.e. the strain gained in this interval divided by the time taken. The interval excludes the start-up transient (rise of the incident pulse, settling of the specimen shoulders in the pockets, build-up of the specimen force up to its maximum at about 5 % strain) and the end of the loading, so that it covers the established regime described by the striker velocity estimate (section 2). The complete strain rate history is given in F4.

![F2](F2_forces_UT19.png)

**Figure F2 (UT19)** - Forces at the specimen input and output sections and force measured by the output bar gauge, shifted to the specimen.

- **UT19, 100 s⁻¹** (striker 2.48 m/s): 30 % strain is **not reached**: the strain stops at 18.5 % when the 2.12 ms pulse ends (105 s⁻¹). Force equilibrium (|R| < 5 %) is established at 0.9 % strain (0.29 ms after loading start) and holds until the end of the useful window (max |R| after 5 % strain: 0.9 %).
- **UT19, 500 s⁻¹** (striker 7.69 m/s): 30 % strain reached 0.63 ms after loading start, at 546 s⁻¹. Force equilibrium (|R| < 5 %) is established at 2.5 % strain (0.12 ms after loading start) and holds until the end of the useful window (max |R| after 5 % strain: 3.8 %).
- **UT19, 1000 s⁻¹** (striker 14.20 m/s): 30 % strain reached 0.33 ms after loading start, at 1073 s⁻¹. Force equilibrium is **not maintained**: after 5 % strain |R| reaches 13.2 % (|F_in - F_out| up to 0.60 kN, i.e. 8.0 % of the maximum force).

In all cases the first few tens of microseconds show a short force pulse followed by a drop to zero before continuous loading starts (F2). This is most likely the impact of the specimen shoulders in the clamping pockets, the specimen being inserted without pre-load; it produces the large values of R below about 2 % strain in F1.

Since the force decreases after the maximum (necking), the same force difference gives a larger ratio R late in the test. The maximum absolute difference |F_in - F_out| after 5 % strain is therefore also given in Table T1.

- UT19, 100 s⁻¹: maximum force 7.50 kN at 5.8 % strain; the output bar gauge deviates from the specimen force by up to 3.1 % of the maximum force after 5 % strain.
- UT19, 500 s⁻¹: maximum force 7.56 kN at 5.0 % strain; the output bar gauge deviates from the specimen force by up to 15.0 % of the maximum force after 5 % strain.
- UT19, 1000 s⁻¹: maximum force 7.58 kN at 7.6 % strain; the output bar gauge deviates from the specimen force by up to 38.9 % of the maximum force after 5 % strain.

The force in the specimen itself is smooth, whereas the output bar gauge signal oscillates around it, with an amplitude that grows with the loading speed. These oscillations are generated between the specimen and the gauge (clamping head of the output bar) and would be present in the measured stress-strain curve.

### Loading speeds

![F4](F4_strain_rate.png)

**Figure F4** - Strain rate (extensometer) as a function of the engineering strain; dotted lines: target rates.

![F5](F5_striker_velocity.png)

**Figure F5** - Striker velocity (vertical axis, setting of the test) required for a given strain rate (horizontal axis). Line: estimate used to set up the simulations. Dots: simulations, placed at the striker velocity imposed and at the strain rate achieved (slope between 5 % and 25 % strain, see section 1).

The striker velocity is estimated from the elongation rate of the load inversion device, v ≈ 2 v_i - 2 F / (ρ c A), where v_i = 0.487 V_striker is the particle velocity of the incident wave and F the specimen force: the specimen force both slows the inverter and pushes the output bar. With an effective length of 12.7 mm and F = 7 kN for the UT19 specimen this gives V_striker = (ε̇ × 12.7 mm + 2F/ρcA) / (2 × 0.487). With the 5 m striker the loading pulse lasts 2.12 ms, so 30 % strain requires at least 0.30 / 2.12 ms ≈ 142 s⁻¹ (shaded region in F5).

## 3. Limitations

- Rate-independent specimen material: in the experiments the force level, and hence the required striker velocity, will increase with the strain rate.
- The output bar gauge signal is shifted, not corrected for dispersion; the exact reference of the 400 mm gauge distance is to be confirmed (about 6 µs uncertainty).
- Numerical quality: the hourglass energy stays below 0.5 % of the internal energy in all cases.
